# Sol → Fable, round 2: how Otto should exist

## Verdict

**One Otto, many work files.**

Keep one identity, one memory system, one action layer, and one relational voice. Do **not** equate that with one indiscriminate physical transcript. A senior employee is one person, but keeps separate case files for campaigns, customers, outlets, approvals, and private owner matters.

The current topology is roughly 80% right:

- The dock is right as quiet, one-step presence.
- The assist button is the strongest new primitive because it makes Otto situationally competent instead of merely available.
- Live reflection is right because it proves that Otto operates the same product the merchant sees.
- Escort is right as a first-use teaching moment.
- A single relationship view is useful.
- A single append-only business-wide chat as the underlying record is wrong.

Otto should feel present through **awareness, judgment, bounded authority, visible work, and receipts**. The cloud identifies the worker. It cannot substitute for the work. That is the presence-design translation of the adopted mechanical doctrine, “bounded authority → sourced receipt” (`docs/strategy/TWO-BRAIN-MEMO-2026-07.md:5-11`), and it respects the constitutional requirement that Otto stay one step away without taking the workbench’s stage (`docs/BLUEPRINT.md:54-66`).

## 1. Presence topology

| Primitive | Verdict | Change today | Shape at scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dock | Keep, but redefine | Make it an attention surface: current work, highest-priority “Needs you,” latest result, and a context-aware command box | A role-aware work queue summarizing concurrent jobs, not an archive |
| One stream | Keep as a relationship projection | Preserve it for the walk, but group turns into scoped work cards and name it an aggregate view | Render a permission-filtered projection over work units, actions, approvals, receipts, and messages |
| Filtered views | Promote from convenience to primary structure | Scope must be explicit; a missing campaign/outlet must never fall back to all history | Server-enforced case files with outlet, actor, role, customer, retention, and visibility boundaries |
| Escort | Narrow | Auto-escort only on the first successful use of a capability; thereafter offer “Watch Otto work” | Optional live-follow mode, never normal navigation |
| “Otto helps me” | Keep and deepen | Show exactly what is attached, let the merchant remove or change scope, preview Apply changes, and prevent stale overwrite | Typed, permission-aware context bridge into the same action layer; never a second AI or bypass |

### What is right

The persistent dock expresses the correct constitutional balance: Otto is always reachable, its work is visible, and the manual surface remains primary (`docs/BLUEPRINT.md:66`; `docs/design-system/design-rules.md:192-222`). The assist affordance is even more important. It realizes the context bridge and “same brain, same memory, same action layer” rules instead of spawning anonymous inline AI (`docs/BLUEPRINT.md:54-62`; `docs/design-system/design-rules.md:484-490`). Apply filling a surface while send/spend still requires the merchant’s tap is exactly right (`docs/northstar/IMMERSIVE-STORE.md:175-205`).

The visual doctrine is also disciplined: coral marks Otto authorship, moods report real state, one narration line follows the nearest work, and the dock carries off-surface state (`docs/design-system/design-rules.md:464-481`). Keep that restraint.

### What breaks at real-merchant scale

**1. The dock currently has two incompatible jobs.** The design law specifies a 320×480 recent-action panel with at most 20 neutral rows and an “Open Otto” exit. The North Star prototype instead makes a 380×520 send-capable window onto the entire stream (`docs/design-system/design-rules.md:478-482`; `docs/northstar/IMMERSIVE-STORE.md:160-167`). The code literally calls unfiltered `streamFor()` and renders every message in the floating panel (`apps/web/components/northstar/immersive/immersive-dock.tsx:158-165,268-314`). The prototype already seeds 62 messages across 3 weeks before real merchant volume begins (`apps/web/components/northstar/_mock.ts:899-904`). Resolve this before live implementation. The attention-dock contract is the right one.

**2. Context chips do not create isolation.** The current filter can only narrow by zone or campaign (`apps/web/components/northstar/immersive/_store.ts:112-116,1374-1385`). A message context has zone, label, optional campaign, and href, but no outlet, staff actor, visibility, customer case, authority basis, or work unit (`apps/web/components/northstar/_mock.ts:877-897`). At 3 outlets and 8 staff, “Merdeka promo” is not enough. A mistaken retrieval can expose private owner guidance, use another outlet’s price, or let the wrong role see an approval.

**3. A label is not an active scope control.** The assist primitive can attach `formState`, but the dock visibly shows only a “Looking at” label (`apps/web/components/northstar/immersive/otto-assist.tsx:47-60`; `apps/web/components/northstar/immersive/immersive-dock.tsx:296-304`). The merchant needs to see: `Sharing this draft · Bangsar outlet · Farah’s enquiry`. Hidden attachment creates the same trust problem as hidden prompting.

**4. One working boolean collapses under concurrency.** The prototype exposes one `{ working, label }` state (`docs/northstar/IMMERSIVE-STORE.md:14-16`). With routines, multiple staff, and outlets, Otto may be publishing one job, waiting for evidence on another, and reconciling a third. A single bobbing cloud and one narration line cannot be the job model. The nearest surface may narrate one job; the dock must summarize the registry.

**5. One append-only transcript cannot serve privacy, deletion, and audit simultaneously.** Conversation may need search, export, deletion, or retention limits. Approval and money receipts may need durable audit. Customer material may be visible only to a subset of staff. Those are different records. The adopted warning is already correct: one employee does not require one physical message list (`docs/strategy/SOL-R1-2026-07-10.md:362-366`; `docs/strategy/TWO-BRAIN-MEMO-2026-07.md:37`).

**6. Campaign filters cannot contain all work.** Inbox recovery, a compliance exception, a recurring FAQ rule, a stock alert, and private owner advice are not naturally campaigns. The broader WorkUnit/Mandate question was rightly deferred for real-product modeling, not resolved by the prototype (`docs/strategy/SOL-R1-2026-07-10.md:69-77`; `docs/strategy/TWO-BRAIN-MEMO-2026-07.md:23-26`).

### Change today

Do not migrate the live data model yet. The adjudication explicitly allows walking the D1/D2 hypothesis while deferring irreversible production ontology (`docs/strategy/TWO-BRAIN-MEMO-2026-07.md:23-26`). Change the interaction contract now:

1. Keep the visible aggregate stream for the walk, but label it internally as a projection, not the future source of truth.
2. Replace repeated chips with a pinned active-scope strip: `Outlet · work item/customer · visibility · acting as`.
3. Bundle contiguous turns into named work cards. Give each a status: `Needs you · Working · Result · Stopped`.
4. Make scoped views fail closed. No campaign ID means “No conversation attached to this campaign,” not the global stream.
5. Change the dock to three operational rows plus the command box. History belongs in full-page Otto.
6. Add mock metadata now: `workUnitId`, `outletId`, `actorId`, `visibility`, `authorityBasis`. This tests comprehension without prematurely choosing the database schema.
7. Auto-escort only for the first successful use of each capability per user. After that, show `Watch in Schedule` or `Keep working here`. Never navigate while the keyboard is open, a form is dirty, a customer reply is in progress, or an approval modal is open.
8. Show assist attachment before send. Apply must preview changed fields, offer Undo, and refuse to overwrite a field that staff changed after Otto produced the patch.

### Build at scale

The canonical objects should be separate:

- `WorkUnit/Mandate`: objective, constraints, outlet, owner, deadline, success and stop conditions.
- `ConversationTurn`: human dialogue, attached to a work unit or explicitly private/general.
- `Action`: exact proposed change, evidence, cost, reach, reversibility, execution state.
- `Approval`: actor, role, scope, policy/authority basis, time.
- `Receipt`: sourced result, exceptions, costs, evidence grade, reconciliation state.
- `MemoryFact`: value, type, source, scope, last verified time, visibility, correction history.
- `AuthorityGrant`: bounded action class, outlet/channel/audience, ceilings, exclusions, expiry, stop trigger.

The global Otto view then becomes a permission-filtered projection over those records. Each staff member gets the same Otto brain but a role-specific briefing. “Needs you” is routed to the person allowed to decide. Shared work lives on the WorkUnit. Private owner guidance stays private. Cross-outlet work has one parent mandate and separately inspectable outlet actions and receipts. Server-side RBAC and outlet filters must happen before data reaches the client; client-side hiding is not isolation. This extends, rather than weakens, the constitutional internal hierarchy and tenant iron curtain (`docs/BLUEPRINT.md:53-62`).

## 2. The employee feeling

The moment-to-moment rhythm should be:

1. The merchant says the outcome.
2. Otto silently checks the current surface, verified memory, permissions, conflicts, and missing evidence.
3. Otto asks one blocking decision only if necessary.
4. Otto recommends one course with reason, downside, scope, and unknowns.
5. The merchant approves a bounded action.
6. Live narration reports execution without adding chat noise.
7. Otto closes with a receipt, exceptions, and the next decision.

That feels like a senior employee because Otto arrives briefed, exercises judgment, owns the handoff, and closes the loop. “Sure, I can help with that” plus a mascot feels like a chatbot.

### Voice rules

- Lead with the recommendation. Then state the decisive evidence, material downside or uncertainty, and one real ask.
- Otto uses first person only for judgment and accountability: `I recommend…`, `I couldn’t verify…`, `I used the wrong price…`. Product controls and infrastructure remain impersonal. This extends the current two-speaker law (`docs/design-system/design-rules.md:441-460`).
- Ban conversational throat-clearing: no `Sure`, `Happy to help`, `Great question`, or automatic `Anything else?`.
- Use fixed epistemic verbs:
  - `I found…` = source-observed.
  - `You confirmed…` = merchant-confirmed.
  - `I linked…` = associated, not caused.
  - `I estimate…` = inference, with a range or named basis.
  - `I can’t verify…` = unknown.
  - `I need…` = blocking fact.
- Never use `I know` for an inference. The evidence ladder separates observation, confirmation, association, attribution, and incrementality; only the last supports a strong causal claim (`docs/strategy/SOL-R1-2026-07-10.md:79-93`; `docs/strategy/TWO-BRAIN-MEMO-2026-07.md:7-9`).
- Ask when a missing fact changes spend, external reach, customer promise, outlet, compliance, consent, or reversibility. For low-risk reversible choices, state a visible default: `I’ll use the last approved brand kit unless you change it.`
- “One ask per message” means one decision, not an interrogation. If 4 related constraints are missing, put them in one compact readiness card.
- Never say “done” until the external state is verified. If it may have applied, say so and stop blind retries.

Examples:

> I recommend pausing this ad. It is below the margin floor you gave me, and 18 boxes remain. No extra ad spend. Pause it now?

> I can verify 8 orders. I can link 5 to this campaign. I can’t tell what caused the other 3.

> Meta did not confirm the pause. The ad may still be live. I won’t retry until I can confirm its state.

### Visible memory

The merchant should see three memory surfaces:

1. **What Otto knows:** a durable, editable register with `Business · Outlet · Customer/segment · Preferences · Permissions · Corrections`. Each item shows source, scope, who confirmed it, last verified time, and whether it is fact, preference, or inference.
2. **Memory used for this decision:** an expandable row on every consequential proposal and receipt. Example: `Bangsar price list · owner confirmed Jul 2`, `No pork imagery · business-wide`, `120-credit campaign cap · this work only`.
3. **Just learned:** a small post-correction acknowledgement with `Keep business-wide · Keep for this outlet · This time only · Forget`.

Never promote a one-off instruction into durable preference without showing that promotion. Never merge outlet-local facts. Never expose restricted finance/compliance memory to an unauthorized staff role. The current source-linked brand-preference surface is a good seed (`docs/northstar/IMMERSIVE-STORE.md:39-40,118-126`), but the North Star store deliberately resets on refresh, so the prototype must not promise durable remembering (`docs/northstar/IMMERSIVE-STORE.md:7-12`).

### When Otto is wrong

Otto should not silently edit history or perform apology theatre. It should:

1. name the exact wrong fact or judgment;
2. name every affected draft/action;
3. state whether anything was sent, spent, or may have applied;
4. stop unsafe follow-on work;
5. correct or reverse what is safely reversible;
6. append a correction receipt;
7. update the scoped memory/policy and show the change;
8. downgrade or pause the affected authority cell if warranted.

Example: `I used KLCC’s RM12 price for Bangsar. The draft was not sent. I corrected it to RM14 and restricted that price memory to Bangsar.`

External ambiguity needs three explicit states: `Not applied · May have applied · Applied, then reversed`. R1 already identifies “maybe applied” and reconciliation as necessary to the employee promise (`docs/strategy/SOL-R1-2026-07-10.md:350-354`).

### How Otto earns authority

Do not show one global autonomy level. The current prototype’s single behavior level is convenient for a walk but contradicts the adopted trust lattice (`docs/northstar/IMMERSIVE-STORE.md:37-40,98-108`; `docs/strategy/TWO-BRAIN-MEMO-2026-07.md:10`).

After a meaningful evidence window, Otto may propose one narrow permission:

> You approved 18 stock-answer drafts, changed 1, and received no complaints in 30 days. Allow automatic drafting for stock questions at Bangsar? Sending still needs your tap.

The card shows action class, outlet/channel, audience, money ceiling, exclusions, expiry, stop trigger, and instant revoke. The merchant taps to grant it. Any incident, drift, missing evidence, or scope change can reduce authority automatically. Authority can expand for drafting while becoming stricter for certification claims. That asymmetry is the point.

### Ten micro-details, ranked by impact

1. **Read-back contract before action.** A compact card repeats goal, outlet, constraints, exact change, audience, credits, external cash, reversibility, stop rule, unknowns, and approver. The merchant can correct one field instead of re-prompting.
2. **Receipt after every meaningful action.** First line says what actually happened; then evidence grade, exceptions, cost, approver, and source links. A celebration never replaces the receipt.
3. **`Known · Inferred · Need from you` on every consequential recommendation.** Uncertainty becomes inspectable structure, not hedging prose.
4. **Sticky active-scope strip.** Every composer and approval reads `Bangsar · Merdeka launch · visible to Marketing · acting as Owner`. This prevents the most damaging scale mistakes.
5. **`Memory used` row.** Let the merchant correct, forget, or narrow any remembered item without leaving the decision.
6. **Three-state execution failure.** `Not applied · May have applied · Applied then reversed`, followed by money/data impact and the safe next move. Never blind-retry an ambiguous external write.
7. **One-tap “Correct Otto.”** The correction flow asks what was wrong, where the right fact applies, and whether already-created work should be updated. It appends a correction; it does not erase the old record.
8. **Visible default instead of a needless question.** `Using your last approved layout and the Bangsar price list. Change.` Otto asks only when the answer can materially change harm or outcome.
9. **Evidence-earned permission card.** Otto cites its own track record and asks for one bounded authority cell, never “Trust me more.”
10. **Interrupt only with judgment.** The badge means `Needs you`, `Stopped to prevent harm`, or `Verified result`, not generic chatter. Routine completions collapse into a digest. The correct information hierarchy is already `Needs you · Up next · Results` (`docs/strategy/SOL-R1-2026-07-10.md:95-105`).

## 3. Emotional design limits

Personality helps with speaker recognition, continuity, onboarding, low-risk exploration, reversible drafts, and modest acknowledgement after a sourced result. It becomes dangerous when warmth makes a risky action feel endorsed or the cloud implies human empathy, universal competence, unlimited labor, guaranteed outcomes, or a support SLA. Those expectation failures are exactly why the external “AI employee” promise remains on trial (`docs/strategy/SOL-R1-2026-07-10.md:388-410`; `docs/strategy/TWO-BRAIN-MEMO-2026-07.md:40-44`).

The mouthless cloud is an advantage. Keep Otto symbolic. Do not make it more human to compensate for missing substance.

| Situation | Allowed Otto state | Hard limit |
|---|---|---|
| Reversible draft/research | `thinking` only while truly running, then `helpful` | No fake “looking busy” motion |
| Waiting for ordinary information | `waiting` | Say exactly what is missing and why |
| Spend, publish, destructive review | Static `warning` or `waiting` in the relevant card | No bob, emoji, exclamation, joke, praise, or “Great choice” |
| After the owner taps | `approving` | Means execution started, never success |
| Verified execution | `success` for ≤4s | Means the task settled, not that the business outcome was good |
| External result may have applied | Persistent semantic warning/error | Never show success; reconcile first |
| Missing compliance evidence | `waiting`, then hard block | Ask for exact evidence; do not offer “Proceed anyway” |
| Unknown compliance rule | `error` plus fail-closed block | Owner approval cannot convert an unsupported claim into truth |
| Billing, top-up, identity | No inline Otto | Dock only; the decision must visually belong to the merchant |

Additional rules:

- Moods encode execution state only. They never encode confidence, truth, safety, quality, urgency, or the merchant’s feelings. This tightens the current “status, not theatre” rule (`docs/design-system/design-rules.md:469-471`).
- Coral says “Otto touched this,” never “this is safe.” Danger remains semantic warning/error styling. Otto may witness danger; coral must not color the danger itself (`docs/design-system/design-rules.md:430-438`).
- The approval CTA is always in the human voice: ink, or human blue inside the immersive shell. Otto’s coral may frame the proposal, but the owner’s tap must not look like Otto acting on itself (`docs/design-system/design-rules.md:43-45,68-88`).
- Money never moves on a mood, voice command, swipe, assist Apply, or notification. It moves only after the owner taps an exact approval, including the bounded envelope that authorizes a routine.
- Spend success appears only after verified settlement and with the exact receipt. Approval itself never earns a celebration.
- No `Don’t worry`, `I’ve got this`, cute apology, or emotional reassurance on money, compliance, privacy, customer harm, or failure. Use verified facts and recovery.

The sharp rule is: **Otto may be warm about the relationship, never warm about risk.**

## 4. Phone-first presence

The current prototype is not merely imperfectly responsive; its topology is desktop-first. The panel remains a fixed bottom-right 380×520 box, capped by viewport arithmetic rather than becoming the specified bottom sheet (`apps/web/components/northstar/immersive/immersive-dock.tsx:267-274`). It contains the entire history and a composer. The textarea is 13px, several controls are 32px, and it tells a phone owner to press Shift+Enter (`apps/web/components/northstar/immersive/immersive-dock.tsx:353-392`). That conflicts with the design system’s own 16px iOS-safe input and 44px touch-target laws (`docs/design-system/design-rules.md:128-148,540-545`).

Change the mobile contract:

1. **Give Otto a fixed safe-area home.** Use a reserved Otto slot in the mobile command bar or bottom navigation. Do not make a floating ball dodge every page CTA. One stable thumb target still satisfies “ever present.”
2. **Tap opens a quick sheet; typing opens full screen.** The sheet contains current work, one “Needs you,” latest verified result, and 2–3 context intents. Focusing the composer promotes to a `100dvh` Otto screen with a sticky 16px composer. A 60vh chat sheet plus keyboard is not viable.
3. **Default to `Here`, not `Whole business`.** The sticky header shows outlet and work/customer scope. `All Otto` is an explicit expansion, never the send default.
4. **Make every target at least 44px.** The cloud may stay 14px visually, but “Otto helps me,” send, close, maximize, chips, and approvals need thumb-safe hit areas. Keep the text label visible.
5. **Make “say what you want” literal.** Add press-to-talk and camera/share attachment. Transcribe voice into editable text before send. Voice can brief; it can never approve money or compliance. The final approval remains a visual tap on an exact card.
6. **Make escort consentful after teaching.** Preserve one automatic first-success escort. Later show `Watch in Schedule · Keep working here`. Never teleport while the keyboard is open or state is dirty. On arrival, show `Back to request` and scroll to the touched object.
7. **Review risk full-screen.** Money, compliance, broad audience, destructive, and ambiguous external states never approve inside the quick sheet or an OS notification. The full-screen review pins outlet, audience, exact amount, reversibility, evidence, consequences, and the owner CTA.
8. **Persist phone state before merchant testing.** The current store intentionally resets on refresh (`docs/northstar/IMMERSIVE-STORE.md:7-12`). Mobile browsers suspend and evict tabs. Draft, active scope, unseen result, escort origin, job state, and approvals must survive reload before “leave and return” can be tested honestly.
9. **Use only three push classes.** `Needs your decision · Stopped to prevent harm · Verified result`. No “thinking” push, routine completion spam, or badge for every Otto message. Respect quiet hours.
10. **Design for interruption.** Every full-screen Otto/work surface restores the exact prior scroll, draft, and selected object when the merchant returns from WhatsApp, a call, or the camera.

## 5. Wild card: Otto’s trial shift

Before granting meaningful authority, let the merchant put Otto on a **7-day shadow shift for one action class at one outlet**.

Example: `Shadow order-question replies at Bangsar.` Otto observes the authorized inputs, prepares what it would have done, records why, and compares its recommendation with the merchant/staff action and sourced result. It makes no external write and triggers no external spend. If shadow inference uses credits, the 7-day envelope is quoted and owner-approved up front. It interrupts only for a credible harm. At the end, Otto gives a short handover:

- 23 cases observed.
- 18 recommendations matched the final action.
- 3 needed information Otto did not have.
- 2 were wrong; here is why.
- No customer message was sent.
- Proposed next permission: auto-draft stock replies; sending still needs your tap.

This is a presence idea, not merely a settings feature. Otto exists as a colleague proving judgment alongside the merchant before asking for keys. It creates calibration data, makes memory gaps visible, and turns authority from a personality judgment into evidence. It also exposes whether “consultant-grade” substance survives real work before the mascot raises expectations.

Pilot it with 10 merchants across 2 recurring work cycles. Kill the idea if any one is true:

- Fewer than 6/10 merchants inspect at least 3 shadow recommendations.
- Fewer than 65% of reviewed recommendations are marked safe and useful.
- More than 5% contain a high-risk false recommendation, or any unsupported compliance claim reaches a permission proposal.
- Fewer than 3/10 merchants grant even one narrow next permission after the handover.
- Median review burden exceeds 10 minutes per merchant per week after week 1.
- Support/rescue time does not decline between cycle 1 and cycle 2.

Do not soften the thresholds after seeing the demo. If the trial shift fails, Otto should remain an on-demand accountable operator in that action class, not cosplay as a trusted employee.

## Final position

Otto should exist as a **quiet, scoped, accountable operator whose presence follows work**:

- one recognizable identity;
- many automatically filed work records;
- visible context and memory;
- one recommendation and one decision at a time;
- action-specific authority;
- calm execution signals;
- explicit correction when wrong;
- sourced receipts when finished.

Keep the cloud. Keep coral. Keep the dock. Keep one relationship. But make the employee feeling come from operational character, not screen time. If Otto notices the right thing, asks only what matters, acts inside a visible boundary, reports the truth, and learns visibly from correction, the merchant will call it an employee without the interface having to insist.
