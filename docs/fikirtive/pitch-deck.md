## Slide 5b — The Wedge Logic

# Why we lead with video ads — and why that's *not* what we sell

**Why THIS wedge (the door / 敲门砖):**
- **Most frequent, most painful job** — every DTC merchant needs fresh short-form video *every week*; creative fatigue forces constant refresh. High frequency → they come back → **retention**.
- **Fastest "wow" for a non-technical merchant** — via Otto, *product link in → ad-pack out in ~10 minutes*. No studio to learn. Instant time-to-value → **activation**.
- **Closest to the money** — video ads sit right next to ad spend and revenue, so expanding into Publish → Ads → Attribution is a *natural next step, not a leap*.

**Why the wedge is the HOOK, not the business:**
- Video generation is **commoditizing to free** (Pippit, Canva, TikTok Symphony). We will **never** make our money on video quality or price.
- We use the wedge to get the merchant — and their **Brand Brain** — *into* Fikirtive.

**Come for the video ads → stay for the OS (land-and-expand):**
- Once the Brand Brain (voice, catalog, customers, what-converts) lives here and Otto is their daily operator, leaving means rebuilding their whole marketing operation. **That is the lock-in.**
- The wedge is the cost-heavy line (video); the expansion (CS / CRM / attribution) is cheap, high-margin software → **margin expands as merchants go deeper** (see Unit Economics).

> *Speaker note:* The wedge isn't the product — it's the cheapest, fastest, most painful door into the product. We give away a great hook on something that's commoditizing anyway, and we monetize the OS the hook leads into. "Come for the ads, stay for the operating system."

## Slide 8 — Business Model

# How we make money

- **Subscription + credits** (+ a minor storage fee). **No % of ad-spend** — that protects merchant trust and avoids the agency conflict.
- **Credits are COGS-indexed** — video (priced per model-second, the dominant cost) drives the credit price; experimentation stays cheap; merchants pay for what they ship.
- **Messaging (WhatsApp/SMS) is pass-through**, billed to the merchant at cost-plus — **never on our P&L** (it's the most volatile cost line in the stack; keeping it off-book is what keeps margin stable).
- **The 10x principle:** Otto must provably earn the merchant **>10× what they pay**.
- **Land-and-expand:** post-wedge modules are cheap software while ARPU nearly triples — **gross margin expands with depth.** NRR > 100% is the **target** this makes reachable (to prove with cohorts, against high SMB churn).

> *Speaker note:* The credit ledger does double duty — it's the pricing model *and* the margin firewall. And by refusing % of ad-spend, we stay the merchant's tool, not their competitor.

## Slide 10 — Cost / Unit Economics

# Unit economics — margin **expands** with depth

**Per-merchant COGS (MYR/mo), as merchants adopt modules:**

| | Wedge | Expanding | Full-product |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Video (largest single line, ~42–53% of COGS; the controllable lever) | 44.0 | 47.0 | 50.0 |
| Otto LLM (Haiku) | 28.0 | 33.0 | 40.0 |
| Other modules (CS/email/KOL/attr/CRM/storage/infra) | ~11.6 | ~20.5 | ~30.2 |
| **Total COGS** | **~83.6** | **~100.5** | **~120.2** |
| **ARPU** | **212** | **400** | **620** |
| **Gross margin** | **~61%** | **~75%** | **~81%** |

- **Why it expands:** the post-wedge modules add only **~RM17** of cheap-software COGS combined (total COGS rises just RM36.6 wedge→full) while ARPU nearly triples. **Incremental margin on expansion ≈ 91%.**
- **The credit/spend-cap ledger is the margin firewall** — included credits are sized so the plan price yields ~70–80% margin; usage above the allotment is billed at cost-plus (heavier users raise their *own* ARPU).
- **Honest stress case (every merchant burns *max* AI at flat ARPU):** fleet margin **36–44%** — the floor the credit cap exists to *prevent*, not the expected case. *We put it on the table on purpose.*

> *Speaker note:* The headline isn't "70% margin" — it's "margin goes *up* as customers stay and adopt more." That's the land-and-expand engine. The 36–44% is the pathological case I'm putting on the table so you trust the rest.

## Slide 12 — Moat / Defensibility

# Defensibility — a head-start, honestly framed

- **It is *not* a data network effect.** That's a scale game Meta and Google already win — we won't pretend otherwise.
- **What's real, and compounding:**
  1. **Workflow & data-gravity lock-in** — once a merchant's Brand Brain, customers, and history live in Fikirtive, leaving is expensive.
  2. **SEA local integrations** — the unglamorous TikTok Shop/Shopee/WhatsApp BSP plumbing competitors skip.
  3. **Closed-loop conversion data, per-merchant** — what converts for *each* merchant's own customers (consented, used to improve *their* results — not a cross-merchant "network effect" we'd have to over-claim).
  4. **The Otto relationship** — the merchant's daily working partner is hard to rip out.

- We call this a **head-start, not a fortress.** Under-claiming it is the credible position — and it's a head-start we widen with every merchant and every integration.

> *Speaker note:* I'd rather lose the deal than oversell the moat. It's a real, compounding head-start built on lock-in, local plumbing, and the Otto relationship — not a magic flywheel.
